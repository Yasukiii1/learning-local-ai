Rainforests

A rainforest is a thick forest that gets a lot of rain, often more than 2,000 millimetres every year. The biggest rainforest is the Amazon in South America.

Rainforests have layers. The emergent layer has the tallest trees. Below it is the canopy, a roof of leaves where most animals live. The understory is dark and quiet. The forest floor gets very little sunlight.

More than half of all the kinds of plants and animals on Earth live in rainforests. Sloths, jaguars, toucans and poison dart frogs all live there.

Rainforest trees make oxygen and take in carbon dioxide. Many medicines come from rainforest plants.

People cut down rainforests for wood, farms and roads. This is called deforestation, and it destroys the homes of many animals.
