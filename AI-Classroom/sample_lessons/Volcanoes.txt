Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust where hot melted rock comes out. Melted rock under the ground is called magma. When magma comes out of a volcano, it is called lava. Lava can be hotter than 1,000 degrees Celsius.

Volcanoes form where tectonic plates move apart or push into each other. The Ring of Fire around the Pacific Ocean has about 450 volcanoes.

An eruption happens when pressure from gas and magma builds up under the ground. Some eruptions are gentle and the lava flows slowly. Other eruptions are explosive and throw out ash and rocks.

A volcano can be active, dormant or extinct. An active volcano has erupted recently. A dormant volcano is sleeping but could erupt again. An extinct volcano will never erupt again.

Volcanic ash makes soil very rich, so farmers often grow crops near volcanoes. Scientists who study volcanoes are called volcanologists.
