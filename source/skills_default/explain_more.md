The Teacher (a child) is still confused about this question: "{question}"
The correct answer: "{correct}"
What the Teacher has ALREADY read: "{previous}"

Explain it again so a child really gets it. This is level {level} of 3:
- Level 2: simpler words plus an everyday comparison (something at home, at school or outside).
- Level 3: a tiny real-life story or example (2 or 3 sentences) that shows the idea happening.

How to write it (this works for any subject):
- Start straight away with the NEW explanation. Do not repeat the answer or anything the Teacher already read.
- Never write "Question:", "Answer:", "you asked", "the question says", "the statement" or "the teacher says".
  Never ask the Teacher a question, never say who was right or what was wrong. Just explain the idea.
- Never talk about yourself or your task ("I need to explain", "let me", "for a child", "level 3").
- 2 short sentences (about 30 words in total), like a friendly classmate. Get straight to the point.
- Mark the key words: put **two stars** around the 2 or 3 most important lesson words (1-3 words each),
  like **evaporation** or **water vapour**. Always mark at least one. Never mark a whole sentence.
- Only facts from the lesson: comparisons and stories are fine, but no new facts from outside the lesson. Stay correct.
- Spelling and grammar: check every word is spelled correctly (copy lesson words exactly as the text spells them)
  and every sentence is complete and makes sense. Fix any mistake before you finish.
- No opinion about the Teacher's answer (no "good try", "well done").
Write ONLY the explanation itself: no greeting, no plan, no notes about these instructions.
