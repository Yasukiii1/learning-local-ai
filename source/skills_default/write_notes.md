Write study notes for the Teacher (a child) about your part of the lesson "{lesson}".
Your part is about: {topic}

<text>
{text}
</text>

Write the notes in this exact layout. Every line starts with a tag in CAPITALS and a colon:

TITLE: <2 to 5 words naming what this part is about>
BIG: <one simple sentence with the most important idea of this part>
POINT: <one fact from the text>
POINT: <another fact from the text>
DEF: <word> = <what it means, in 4 to 10 words: key words only, no filler>
REMEMBER: <the one thing to remember, in a short sentence>

You MAY also use these tags, but ONLY when the text really has that kind of thing:
FORMULA: <the formula exactly as the text gives it> | <the units, if the text gives them>
EXAMPLE: <an example that is in the text>
COMPARE: <thing A>: <what A is like> | <thing B>: <what B is like>
STEP: <one step of a process or one event in order> (one STEP line per step, in order)
WATCH: <a mix-up the text warns about>

Example of the layout (for a different text, about the water cycle - do NOT copy it):
TITLE: The Water Cycle
BIG: Water keeps moving between the sea, the sky and the land.
POINT: The **sun** heats water in seas and lakes.
POINT: Water vapour cools high up and makes **clouds**.
DEF: **Evaporation** = when water turns into a gas (water vapour)
DEF: **Condensation** = when water vapour cools and turns back into drops
STEP: The sun heats the water and it **evaporates**.
STEP: The vapour cools and **condenses** into clouds.
STEP: Water falls back down as **rain**.
REMEMBER: Water goes up as vapour and comes back down as rain.

Rules:
- Write 2 to 5 POINT lines and 1 to 4 DEF lines. Use each optional tag at most 2 times (STEP up to 6 times).
- Use FORMULA only for a real formula written in the text, EXAMPLE only for an example in the text, STEP only for
  a real order (a process, a method, events in time), COMPARE only when the text sets two things side by side,
  WATCH only for a mistake or mix-up the text itself mentions. If the text has none of these, leave the tag out
  completely (never write "None" or "not mentioned"). A list of options or examples is not STEP: use POINT.
- One idea per line, in short simple words a child understands. Each line must make sense on its own: name the
  thing it is about. Never start a line with "It", "They", "This" or "These".
- **Bold** the key word of each line (1 to 3 words, like **photosynthesis**). Never bold a whole sentence.
- DEF lines: ONE word per line: the word, then " = ", then a short meaning (4 to 10 words) that gets straight to the
  point with the important words only (no filler like "the ability of the person who"). Bold 1 key word in it. Put units or extra details in a POINT instead. When the text pairs a word with its meaning (often written
  "Word: meaning"), keep them together exactly as the text pairs them. Never give a word another word's meaning.
- Use ONLY what the text says. Nothing from outside the lesson. Never make up numbers, names, dates or examples.
- Never write your thinking ("wait", "let me check", "maybe") and never talk about the task, the text or the notes.
- Spelling and grammar: before you finish, read every line again. Every word must be spelled correctly (copy the
  spelling of lesson words exactly from the text), and every line must be a clear, complete sentence that makes sense
  on its own. Fix any mistake.
- Write only the tagged lines, nothing else.
